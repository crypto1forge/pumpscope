Pump setups

Each pumped coin gets an exhaustion score (short and fade evidence) and a continuation score (reasons not to short). A short trigger needs a confirmed break of structure, so the page does not tell you to short strength.
Shorting pumped small caps can lose several times your stake when a squeeze hits, funding can cost you while you wait, and thin books gap through stops. Size from the stop distance, never from conviction. Scores are rules of thumb that start untested; the journal below measures whether they work for you. Not financial advice.

Signal journal

Recent signals

Outcomes are replayed from 5-minute candles after each signal: win if price reaches the target before the stop, loss if the stop is hit first (a candle touching both counts as a loss), otherwise closed at 24 hours. Results include 0.08% for fees but not funding or slippage. Fewer than 30 resolved signals is mostly noise.

Exchange scanner

Vol surge = recent volume divided by normal volume. For the 10 best candidates per market on Binance, Bybit and OKX it compares the last 15 minutes with the previous ~2 hours of 5-minute candles. Everything else uses the change in 24h volume between scans, so those figures appear after the second refresh. "Volume surge ↑" means volume is well above normal while price is rising. It flags activity to look at, not a trade.

Position, margin and cost calculator

Direction comes from the stop: below entry is a long, above is a short. "Value per 1.00 move per unit" is 1 for most stock and index CFDs quoted per unit, or the contract size for lot-based products (for example 100 for a 100 oz gold lot). Check your broker's contract specification. The spread is counted as extra cost on top of the stop distance.

Price alerts for CFDs

The charts and prices above come from TradingView widgets, which this page cannot read, so it cannot watch a CFD price for you. Set price alerts in TradingView or in your broker's platform. If you have a broker or data account with an API, a live feed can be connected later, and the alert engine from the crypto side would then work here too.
Charts, calendar and market data are embedded TradingView widgets and load from TradingView's servers. Some feeds are delayed. Availability of a symbol depends on TradingView.
Data comes straight from free public APIs (CoinGecko, DexScreener, Binance, Bybit, OKX, Bitget, Gate, DeFiLlama, alternative.me and public news RSS). Free tiers are rate limited and a few seconds to minutes behind paid terminals. "Signals" and "Top picks" are simple heuristics that flag unusual activity; they are not proof of why price moved and not financial advice.
